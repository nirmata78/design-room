Clarifying question 1 of 6
How many applications?
2 million a month. The Saturday peak is about ten times the average.
Clarifying question 2 of 6
How long can the customer wait?
Offers on screen in under 3 s for 95 % of applications. 10 s is a lost sale.
Clarifying question 3 of 6
Who is the lender of record?
We decide on the partner bank's credit policy and service the loan. The bank is the lender of record.
Clarifying question 4 of 6
Which vendors, and how fast?
Two credit bureaus: p50 0.6 s, p99 several seconds, about 1 % errors. An identity and fraud vendor, p99 400 ms. Optional bank-account data: seconds to minutes.
Clarifying question 5 of 6
What do the calls cost?
About $1 a bureau pull. Identity checks about 5 cents.
Clarifying question 6 of 6
Which rules apply?
Disclosures before signing. The principal reasons for every decline. Records kept about seven years. Fairness testing of models.

Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is
A system that gathers identity, fraud and credit data from paid vendors under one deadline, returns an explainable decision with priced offers, and books exactly one loan when the customer accepts an offer they were shown.
Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is not

Four things it is confused with

Not a scoring model

The model is one input. Policy decides, and pricing prices. Each is versioned on its own.

Not a payment

No money moves at accept. Funding the merchant happens later, asynchronously.

Not a saga

Only one step must be atomic. A credit pull cannot be compensated: it happened.

Not an underwriting queue

Refer means minutes, by text. A human queue of days is another product.

Theory · 2 of 6 · the properties

State safety and liveness separately

Safety · must never happen

S1 A loan booked twice, or without acceptance of the exact offer and disclosure shown.
S2 A decline or a price without reasons about the applicant that can be reproduced.
S3 Raw identity data outside the vault boundary.
S4 More than one pull per bureau per application.

Liveness · must eventually happen

L1 Every application gets an answer (offer, refer, decline) within the deadline.
L2 Every booked loan is funded and handed to servicing, with one effect.
L3 Every referred application is finished within ten minutes.

Technology · application database

The database decides, in one transaction: application, loan and outbox

How it is builtA unique constraint on the application ID means a second booking cannot exist. State changes are conditional updates that name the state they expect.   calls = {v: start(adapter[v], app)
           for v in required(app)}

    t.on_done(record_late_answer)  # paid: keep

What costs points in this round

Design

Treating it as a throughput problem. Sequential vendor calls. Hedging paid pulls. A saga that compensates a credit pull. Check-then-insert on the loan.

Judgement

“Approve with partial data” as a default. Re-running today's model to explain yesterday. “Encrypt the database” for identity. The card standard quoted for an SSN. No owner for any degraded path.

The close
The core is small and boring on purpose: one database, one atomic step. The design lives at the slow edges and in what we keep: one deadline with a signed policy, a record that replays, identity in a vault.
