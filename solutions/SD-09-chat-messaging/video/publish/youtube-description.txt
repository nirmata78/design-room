A chat system that never loses a message and never shows one twice, for a billion users. A full mock system design round at the Staff bar (L6/L7): interviewer, candidate and coach, 44 minutes, 85 numbered slides, then 5 follow-ups.

The thesis: start from the mailbox, not the socket. A message is durably stored in its conversation log, with a server-assigned sequence number, before anything is acknowledged. Every device then catches up by cursor. The WebSocket only delivers early.

IN THIS ROUND
• Estimates as arithmetic: 100 M sockets, 20 PB a year, the million-receipt trap
• Wire formats for send, deliver and sync
• Data model: Chen ERD, physical tables, wide-column, hot keys and who writes them
• C4 context, container and component diagrams; a sequence and code for each path
• Ordering: one authority per conversation, and leader failover without reordering
• The lost ACK: every crash point, and why dedup comes before numbering
• Groups: shared log vs inbox copies, coalesced inbox writes, receipts as cursors
• A gateway dies with 500 k connections: jitter, resumption, admission control
• Edit and delete as a mid-round requirement; versioning a protocol for slow-updating phones

HOW TO USE IT
Watch once straight through. On the second pass, pause after each question and answer first. Slides badged AUX are the manager layer: skip them in a Staff round.
