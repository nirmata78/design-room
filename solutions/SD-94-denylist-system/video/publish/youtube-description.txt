A denylist looks like a hash set and a lookup. The hard part is everything around it: how a ban reaches 100,000 servers in seconds, what a server does when it cannot hear about new bans, and what stops one wrong line from blocking everyone. This is a full reference solution at the Staff bar (L6/L7): theory, a three-version design, seven challenges that break it, and the build cost. 107 minutes, 599 numbered slides, with an interviewer, a candidate and a coach.

The thesis: the check must be local and the list cannot be. So the design is the distance between them: a version every node can verify, a stated bound on how stale a node may be, and the discipline of a release, because the pipe that delivers a ban in ten seconds delivers a mistake in ten seconds.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates as arithmetic: 40 MB per node, 4 TB against 800 MB of fan-out, 10,000 manifest polls a second, 18 seconds against a 30-second target
• Theory: safety and liveness (a missing ban and a wrong block), seven mechanisms, an options matrix with a pick
• Seven challenges: a node that was offline, order and overlap, a bad push, stale or no control plane, a list that does not fit, a slow external source, and how you would know
• The build cost, in people and months (marked AUX, the manager layer)

HOW TO USE IT
Run the round cold first. Then watch once straight through. On the second pass, pause after each interviewer question and answer it first. Slides badged AUX are the manager layer: skip them in a Staff round.
