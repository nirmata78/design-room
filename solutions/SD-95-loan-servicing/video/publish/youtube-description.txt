600,000 repayments are due on the 1st. It sounds like a thundering herd; it is 42 a second. The hard parts of loan servicing are elsewhere: a card charge that times out, a bank debit that settles and comes back 40 days later, a refund that changes the schedule of a live loan, and a month-end where the ledger says $400 more than the bank. A full reference solution at the Staff bar (L6/L7): theory, a three-version design, eight challenges that break it, and the build cost. 106 minutes, 703 numbered slides, with an interviewer, a candidate and a coach.

The thesis: throughput is never the problem here; correctness over months is. An immutable double-entry ledger, a payment state machine that admits unknown and returned, a scheduler bounded by the payment rail rather than the database, and a daily three-way reconciliation.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates as arithmetic: 42 a second on the busy day, a 22-minute card window under a 100/s contract, $90M on one morning
• Theory: safety and liveness, seven mechanisms, an options matrix (including why a message broker solves the wrong problem)
• Eight challenges: the processor timed out, the morning of the 1st, a debit that settled and came back, a refund mid-loan, a payoff with a debit in flight, $400 more than the bank, the card processor down on the 1st, and how you would know
• The build cost and team seams (marked AUX, the manager layer)
