Requirements · scope

In scope, out of scope

schedule·collect·adjust·returns·reconcile

In scope

  • Schedules and terms versions
  • Bank debit and card collections, retries, returns
  • Refunds, late fees, early payoff
  • The ledger and reconciliation
  • Customer notices, as events

Out of scope, on purpose

  • Origination and underwriting (SD-82)
  • Collections agencies; hardship beyond a pause flag
  • Statement design, tax reporting
  • Payouts to merchants (SD-81)
  • Funding and selling loans

Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is
Turn each loan's terms into a schedule, collect each amount once through rails that answer in days and reverse weeks later, record every movement as balanced, immutable lines, and prove daily the books agree with the bank.
Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is not

Four things it is confused with

Not a payment gateway

A gateway turns one intent into one payment in seconds (SD-44). Servicing drives many payments over months, against a schedule that changes.

Not a cron job

A cron job fires work. It does not know what was attempted, what is pending, or what came back.

A ledger is not a balance table

A balance is a sum of lines. A number updated in place cannot explain itself to an auditor.

Settled is not final

A bank debit can be returned after it settles. Final is a later, rule-defined point.

Theory · 2 of 6 · the two properties

State safety and liveness separately

Safety · must never happen

S1 One instalment collected twice.
S2 A ledger transaction that does not sum to zero in integer cents, or a line that is edited.
S3 A customer penalised beyond policy, or for our failure: fees, re-presentments, late marks.

Liveness · must eventually happen

L1 Every due instalment is attempted on its due date, before the rail’s cut-off.
L2 Every payment leaves UNKNOWN and PENDING for SETTLED or RETURNED, within a bound.
L3 Every difference between ledger, processor and bank becomes an owned break within a day.

  with db.transaction():
    rows = db.claim_due(today, shard, 500) # SKIP LOCKED
    for r in rows:
      i = intents.open(r.instalment)       # one open

def dispatch(i):                           # rail bucket
  try: r = rail.charge(i.token, i.amount, key=i.key)
  except Timeout: return db.set(i, "UNKNOWN")
  ledger.post(i, r, key=i.key)             # one commit

Technology · servicing database

The ledger and the work queue in one transaction

How it is builtLedger entries are double entry: each transaction's lines sum to zero, checked by a deferred constraint at commit. Payment intents are rows with a state, changed by conditional updates. The due-item queue is a table selected with row locks that skip rows already taken. The outbox row commits with the entry. Tables are partitioned by month, so old months become read-only and cheap.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
PostgreSQL 16, partitioned by month, one writer to the ledgerA collection is a state change, a pair of balanced ledger entries and an event. They must commit together, and one writer to the ledger keeps the order simple.
A message broker as the queueA second system to keep consistent with the ledger. The table queue commits with the entry.
Multiple ledger writersOrdering, locking and deadlock handling for no throughput we need.
A key-value storeNo multi-row transaction, so balance and intent could disagree.
Background: theory/03 (partitioning and storage) and theory/07 (correctness under failure)

Technology · dispatcher

A worker that spends each rail's limit on purpose

How it is builtThe dispatcher takes due intents and spends tokens from the rail's bucket. The day is planned backwards from each rail's cut-off, with the largest and riskiest items first and a jittered spread. Each call carries our reference. If a rail is down the bucket stops, and intents wait instead of retrying in a storm.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
Go worker, a token bucket per rail, a plan of the dayA bank-debit rail and a card processor have limits, cut-offs and a cost. Firing everything at midnight on the first would break them.
Firing every due item at onceThe first of the month would breach the bank limit and the processor's rate.
Retrying every failure immediatelyA retry storm on a rail that is already struggling.
Background: theory/08 (reliability and operations)

Technology · file ingest

Settlement and return files stored raw before they are parsed

How it is builtEach file is stored raw in object storage with a hash, then parsed by a batch job. Counts and totals are checked against the file's trailer before any line is applied. Each line is matched to an intent by reference, and applying a return is an idempotent ledger transaction. A file that fails a check is quarantined, not half applied.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
Object storage for raw files, a batch parser, counts and totals checked firstReturns arrive weeks later, in files. A parse bug must never lose the original.
Parsing on arrivalA bug loses the original, and a half-applied file is hard to unwind.
Streaming each line as it comesFiles are daily; a stream adds state for no freshness gain.

Technology · reconciliation

Ledger, rail and bank matched every day, line by line

How it is builtA daily job matches every ledger entry to the rail's settlement line and to the bank statement by reference. Two-way agreement is a match; a line missing on one side, a different amount or a duplicate is a break. Breaks carry an age and an owner, and each resolution is a new ledger entry. A total that differs from the bank's by four hundred dollars is explained line by line, not plugged.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
Batch pipeline over the ledger, the rail's file and the bank's statement, by referenceThe ledger, the rail and the bank are three versions of the truth. Differences are breaks, and each break has an owner.
Matching on amount and date aloneEqual payments on the same day are ambiguous.
A monthly reconciliationBreaks age for weeks and the month-end close becomes an investigation.

OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
Batch pipeline over the ledger, the rail's file and the bank's statement, by referenceThe ledger, the rail and the bank are three versions of the truth. Differences are breaks, and each break has an owner.
Matching on amount and date aloneEqual payments on the same day are ambiguous.
A monthly reconciliationBreaks age for weeks and the month-end close becomes an investigation.
