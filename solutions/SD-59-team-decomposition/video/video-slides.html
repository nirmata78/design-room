Theory · 1 of 5 · what it is
A division of the work into teams that can ship alone, joined by explicit contracts, with one owner for everything shared, and a way to see early that the cut is wrong.
Theory · 1 of 5 · what it is not

Four things it is confused with

Not an org chart

Who reports to whom is one input. A decomposition is also what each team owns and what it promises the others.

Not an architecture diagram

Boxes show components. A decomposition also says which team changes them, and who must agree.

Not a task list

Assigning components to people leaves the arrows unmanaged, which is where the slips are.

Not microservices

Five teams do not need twelve services. A boundary is a cost, and it is expensive to move.

Theory · 2 of 5 · the two properties

State safety and liveness separately

Safety · must never happen

[S1-no-silent-break] A change on one side breaks a consumer, and nobody finds out before release.
[S2-one-owner-each] A component, contract or table has no owner, or two.

Liveness · must eventually happen

[L1-no-team-waits] Every team has unblocked work every week.
[L2-integrated-by-week-4] An end-to-end path runs by week four and stays green.
[L3-wrong-split-seen-early] A wrong cut is visible by month two.
