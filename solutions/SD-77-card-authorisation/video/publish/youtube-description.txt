Card authorisation looks like the easiest question in a fintech loop: a request arrives, you check the balance, you say yes. It is asked at every level because that answer is wrong in three places at once. This is a full reference solution at the Staff bar (L6/L7): theory, a three-version design, eight challenges that break it, and the build cost. 75 minutes, 156 numbered slides, with an interviewer, a candidate and a coach.

The thesis: card authorisation is a promise made inside someone else's deadline. A hold, not a debit. Every network message once, in any order. A written answer for when a step is late. And a ledger that absorbs the decisions the network made for us.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates as arithmetic: 1,200 requests in flight, 12,000 state reads a second, a 7 GB hold book in memory, what a slow fraud model costs per minute, a 300 ms budget with one step allowed to be slow
• Theory: safety and liveness, seven mechanisms, an options matrix for where the decision and the money live, with a pick
• Eight challenges: the tip, two swipes on one balance, the same message twice and out of order, the model slow tonight, a burst seconds apart, a new attack at 2 a.m., twenty minutes of stand-in with a late clearing file, and declines doubling while dashboards stay green
• The build cost and team seams (marked AUX, the manager layer)

HOW TO USE IT
Run the round cold first. Then watch once straight through. On the second pass, pause after each interviewer question and answer it first. Slides badged AUX are the manager layer: skip them in a Staff round.
