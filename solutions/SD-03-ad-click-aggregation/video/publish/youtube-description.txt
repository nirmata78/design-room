Counting clicks sounds like n = n + 1. At thirty million events a second, with retries, phones that were offline for a day and a fraud team that changes its mind a week later, the hard part is counting once, in the right hour, and proving it. A compact reference solution at the Staff bar (L6/L7): theory, a three-version design, an alternate path, and six challenges that break it. 72 minutes, 482 numbered slides.

The thesis: count once, from one log, with one piece of code run twice, fast and provisional for the dashboard, exact and pinned for the bill. Never increment a number you can recompute: replace it, and keep every version you billed.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates from one scale line: 10 M events a second, 6 GB/s at peak, 0.7 TB of dedup state, and $375 M a month in duplicate clicks if retries are not removed
• Theory: five mechanisms (event IDs, watermarks, replace-not-increment, two-stage aggregation, HyperLogLog) and Lambda against Kappa
• Six challenges: retries, late and skewed events, one ad at 2 million a second, a four-hour replay, fraud after the invoice, and a wrong number nobody notices
