A live map of two million vehicles sounds like one database of positions. It's two products off one stream, and it's built from three questions you may already know: event counting, nearby drivers and a device fleet. A compact reference solution at the Staff bar (L6/L7): theory, a three-version design, an alternate path, and six challenges. 60 minutes, 502 numbered slides.

The thesis: one log keyed by vehicle, read twice: an overwrite view by cell for the map, and an append history by vehicle for replay. Sequence numbers decide what is newer; lanes decide what goes first.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates: 1 M positions a second, 400 MB of live state, 52 TB of history a month, 9 M messages after a city-wide outage
• Theory: one log two views, per-vehicle sequence numbers, cells at three resolutions, lanes and backpressure, cell subscriptions, and the seams to SD-03, SD-39 and SD-29
• Six challenges: out of the tunnel, downtown at six, five thousand viewers, a city coming back online, one vehicle's exact hour, and a map that is stale while everything is green
