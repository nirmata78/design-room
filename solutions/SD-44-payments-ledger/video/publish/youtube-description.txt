The happy path of a payment is three boxes and a database. This question is about the other paths: a bank call that times out and leaves you not knowing whether the customer paid, three retries at once, and a statement that disagrees with your books. A compact reference solution at the Staff bar (L6/L7): theory, a three-version design, technology choices against their rivals, an alternate path for a small business, and six challenges that break it. 82 minutes, 549 numbered slides, with an interviewer, a candidate and a coach.

The thesis: exactly-once does not exist across a network boundary. So the design is at-least-once delivery, one effect per intent, a state for not knowing yet, and a reconciliation that finds what the rest missed, on an append-only double-entry journal.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates from one scale line: 9 billion payments a month, 40,000 journal entries a second, 580 TB in seven years
• Why the partition key is the merchant and not the account, and what changes with a million one-time accounts
• Why Go, against Java virtual threads, Spring WebFlux, Python and Rust
• Gateway or direct processor: what a gateway buys, what it costs, the break-even
• Debezium against a polling relay and the other ways to read the log
• Six challenges: the timeout, three retries, a crash before the event, the hot merchant, 999,998 against 1,000,000, and how you would know first
