The happy path of a payment is three boxes and a database. This question is about the other paths: a bank call that times out and leaves you not knowing whether the customer paid, three retries of the same payment at once, a refund ninety days later, and a statement that disagrees with your books. A full reference solution at the Staff bar (L6/L7): theory, a three-version design, eight challenges that break it, and the build cost. 74 minutes, 160 numbered slides, with an interviewer, a candidate and a coach.

The thesis: exactly-once does not exist across a network boundary. So the design is at-least-once delivery, one effect per intent, a state for "we don't know yet", and a reconciliation that finds what the rest missed, all on an append-only double-entry journal where a balance is a sum, never a field.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates as arithmetic: 40,000 journal entries a second, a hot merchant at 5,000 a second against one row lock, a 2-second budget the processor owns
• Theory: safety and liveness, seven mechanisms, an options matrix (including why a distributed transaction with the bank is impossible)
• Eight challenges: the bank timed out, three retries at once, a crash between ledger and event, the hot merchant, refunds and a late chargeback, 999,998 against 1,000,000, a bug that double-posted 40,000 entries, and how you would know
• The build cost and the one-way doors (marked AUX, the manager layer)
