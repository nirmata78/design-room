Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is
One customer intent becomes one movement of money, recorded as balanced, immutable entries, and kept in agreement with the parties that actually move it.
Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is not

Four things it is confused with

The payment system is not the ledger

One orchestrates an intent across outside parties. The other is the book of record. They fail differently.

A ledger is not a balance table

A balance is a sum over entries. A number updated in place cannot explain itself.

Idempotency is not message de-duplication

It is one effect per intent, with the original answer returned to every retry.

Exactly-once delivery is not exactly-once effect

Across a network we can have the second, never the first.

Theory · 2 of 6 · the two properties

Safety and liveness, separately

Safety · must never happen

S1 One intent moves money twice.
S2 A journal transaction that does not balance.
S3 History rewritten: a correction is a new transaction.

Liveness · must eventually happen

L1 Every payment leaves “unknown” for a final state.
L2 Every difference with the outside world becomes an owned break within a day.
L3 Every state change reaches the merchant.

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 1 of 5

M1 · An idempotency key, inserted first

Property it protects

S1: one intent, one effect.

How it works

The client sends a key. We insert it, unique per client, with a hash of the request, in the transaction that creates the payment. The final response is stored on the row.

Failure it rules out

A retry or a concurrent duplicate becoming a second payment; a changed retry silently accepted.

What it costs

A table with a retention window; an “in progress” answer clients must handle.

How it works

PostgreSQL: one merchant's postings stay in one partition

How it is builtThe key is the owner, the merchant, not the account: a capture touches three accounts and would span partitions. Shared accounts, like fee revenue, exist once per partition and are summed for the company view.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
PostgreSQL 16, 64 partitions by merchant, a synchronous standby eachState, journal and outbox commit together or not at all; a posting never leaves its partition.
Partitioning by accountEvery capture becomes a cross-partition transaction.
TigerBeetle, a ledger databaseFastest at debits and credits, but state, keys and outbox need a database anyway. The upgrade past one primary.
A key-value store, or a log as the truthNo multi-row transaction, no synchronous refund limit.

Recap · common mistakes in this round

What costs candidates the most

On correctness

“A distributed transaction with the bank.” A balance column as the truth. A timeout treated as a failure. Floats. Keys checked, not inserted. Partitioning by account.

On operations

Events published outside the transaction. History edited. Reconciliation by totals. No owner for breaks. Dashboards with no money on them.

If you have thirty seconds

What

A state machine per payment, an idempotent API, an immutable double-entry journal partitioned by merchant, reconciliation.

The trade

Pending answers and a reconciliation team, in exchange for never guessing.

The risk

The unknown path runs rarely; test it daily. And the replication slot.

Next

A gateway first if you are small; harden inquiry per processor; make breaks an owned, aged workflow.
