Requirements · scope

In scope, out of scope

lobby and queue·seat map·holds·checkout·release

In scope

  • A lobby and a line in front of the sale
  • A seat map and holds on specific seats
  • Checkout with one external payment provider
  • Expiry, release, and the promoter adding seats
  • Bot controls, in the first version

Out of scope, on purpose

  • Event search and discovery
  • Pricing and dynamic pricing
  • The resale marketplace
  • The refunds interface
  • The mobile app itself
The core
The core is four things: an atomic claim on a three-state seat, a hold with two expiry paths, a waiting room at the edge, and a payment path that treats unknown as a state.

Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is
A fixed set of individually identified seats, sold to many more buyers, with a time-limited hold between free and sold, so no seat is sold twice or stuck.
Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is not

Four things it is confused with

Not a lock

A lock belongs to a process and goes with it. A hold is data with an expiry: it survives a crashed server and needs no open transaction.

Not a shopping cart

A cart promises nothing. A hold promises nobody else can take this seat until a time you can read.

Not an auction

Price does not move with demand. The contest is who gets there first, not who pays most.

Not a queue, as a data structure

The line is a product experience: a position, an estimated wait, an honest likely sold out. It is also a way to shed load.

Theory · 2 of 6 · the properties

State safety and liveness separately

Safety · must never happen

S1 One seat belongs to two orders.
S2 Money is taken for seats we do not deliver, or seats are delivered with no money.

Liveness · must eventually happen

L1 Every seat is eventually sold or free again: nothing stuck in limbo.
L2 Every fan learns where they stand: a position, sold out, an order result.
L3 The sale-opening minute does not take the system down.

Why two safety propertiesA seat can be wrong in two directions: sold twice, and paid for but not delivered. Most designs defend S1 and forget S2.

S1 One seat belongs to two orders.
S2 Money is taken for seats we do not deliver, or seats are delivered with no money.

Liveness · must eventually happen

L1 Every seat is eventually sold or free again: nothing stuck in limbo.
L2 Every fan learns where they stand: a position, sold out, an order result.
L3 The sale-opening minute does not take the system down.

Why two safety propertiesA seat can be wrong in two directions: sold twice, and paid for but not delivered. Most designs defend S1 and forget S2.

One statement is the check and the change

How it is builtEach seat of each event is a row with a state. A claim is one update statement that names the state it expects: set held where the seat is free. A second fan's identical statement matches no row and learns the seat is taken. The hold row and the outbox row commit in the same transaction. Read committed is enough because the check and the change are the same statement.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
PostgreSQL 16, primary and synchronous standby, read committedSelling a seat once is a conditional update on a row, in a transaction with the hold and the outbox. Row locks and constraints are the tools that make it true.
Read the seat, then update itTwo fans both read free and both write. The conditional update closes the gap in one statement.
Redis for the holdA key can expire or vanish while the database still says held. The hold belongs in the same transaction as the seat.
A distributed lock per seatOne more system, and a lock without a token has the stale-holder failure.

A line held in memory, advanced by a pointer

How it is builtA fan joins the lobby and receives a position. An admission pointer advances at the rate the controller sets. A Lua script moves a batch from the lobby into the admitted set and mints their tokens in one atomic step, so no fan is admitted twice. Polling for position is served from a cached snapshot, not from Redis.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
Redis, a sorted set for the lobby, a pointer, Lua scripts for the atomic stepsA million fans arriving at once cannot all reach the shop. The line is a data structure that must be fast and atomic.
The relational database as the queueA million inserts in a minute onto the inventory primary: the line would take the booking path down with it.

The hold expirer sweeps holds past their time and releases them; the claim itself also expires an old hold when it finds one. A reconciler asks the provider about payments that went silent.

Lock in order, claim if free, all or nothing

BEGIN
-- 1. lock the rows, in a stable order; fail now, do not queue
SELECT seat_id FROM event_seat WHERE seat_id = ANY(:seats)
  ORDER BY seat_id FOR UPDATE NOWAIT
-- 2. the WHERE clause IS the availability check
UPDATE event_seat SET state = 'HELD', hold_id = :h, expires_at = now() + 5 min
  WHERE seat_id = ANY(:seats) AND (state = 'AVAILABLE'
        OR (state = 'HELD' AND expires_at < now()))
  RETURNING seat_id
-- fewer rows than asked: ROLLBACK, answer 409 with a fresh bitmap
