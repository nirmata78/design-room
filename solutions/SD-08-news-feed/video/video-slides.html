What makes this problem hard, in words that outlive any product name

Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is
A materialised view per reader: a bounded, ranked list of recent post IDs, part precomputed, part fetched, filtered and hydrated within the budget.
Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is not

Four things it is confused with

Not the source of truth

Posts are. The view may be wrong for seconds, and can be thrown away and rebuilt.

Not a chat inbox

An inbox may never lose a message. A feed may leave out an old post, and that is acceptable.

Not search

It answers one query shape: recent, followed, ranked. Another shape needs another path.

Not the ranking model

Ranking is a stage with a budget, owned elsewhere.

Theory · 2 of 6 · the two properties

State safety and liveness separately

Safety · must never happen

S1 A viewer sees content they must not see (deleted, from a blocked account, outside its audience) for longer than 5 s.
S2 A post is lost: the source of truth is durable even if every view is lost.

Liveness · must eventually happen

L1 A post reaches its active followers' feeds within 5 s, p95.
L2 Every feed open answers within 200 ms, degraded if it must.
L3 A lost or skipped timeline is rebuilt on its next open.

Theory · 3 of 6 · the central tension

Write amplification against read cost

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 1 of 7

Fan-out on write into bounded ID lists

Property it protects

L1 and L2: fresh, and one read per open.

How it works

On each post, insert (post ID, author, time) into each active follower's sorted list, trimmed to 500. Keyed by post ID, so a repeat is a no-op.

Failure it rules out

A feed open that touches 200 authors; duplicate entries when an event is delivered twice.

What it costs

120,000 inserts a second, a memory tier, and amplification on big authors.

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 2 of 7

Fan-out on read from author indexes

Property it protects

L2 for celebrities, and L3: rebuild.

How it works

At read time, read each pulled author's recent post IDs from a per-author index, and merge. The same path rebuilds a lost timeline and answers catch-up.

Failure it rules out

A hundred million writes for one post; a lost view staying lost.

What it costs

A merge per read. The author indexes must be fast, and protected from a stampede.

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 3 of 7

A threshold, with transition rules and active-only targeting

Property it protects

L1, and cost.

How it works

Each author has a tier: push below the line, pull above it, push to recently active followers in the middle. The line has hysteresis, and crossing it needs no backfill.

Failure it rules out

Pure push's celebrity, pure pull's read cost, and writes to users who never read.

What it costs

A tier per author to maintain, and a number to tune and defend.

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 4 of 7

IDs in the view, hydration through a post cache

Property it protects

S1 (one place to delete), and memory.

How it works

The view holds IDs only. The read path multi-gets the top 50 bodies from a post cache backed by the posts store.

Failure it rules out

250 TB of copies; an edit or delete chasing millions of entries.

What it costs

A multi-get per page, and a post cache that becomes critical.

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 5 of 7

A visibility filter on the read path

Property it protects

S1.

How it works

Every page drops posts that are deleted or out of audience at hydration, and authors in the reader's block, mute and unfollow set at merge.

Failure it rules out

A deleted or blocked post surviving in a precomputed copy.

What it costs

A small per-reader set read on every open.

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 6 of 7

Ranking under a deadline, with a fallback

Property it protects

L2.

How it works

Call ranking with a hard deadline. On a miss, order by recency and close ties, or reuse the session's last ranking, and count it.

Failure it rules out

A slow model making the whole feed slow or failing.

What it costs

Lower quality while degraded, which must be measured and owned.

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 7 of 7

Log-based fan-out with priority lanes

Property it protects

L1 under bursts.

How it works

The post and its event commit together (an outbox); a log keyed by author feeds the workers; close ties are fanned out first; the offset is committed last.

Failure it rules out

A burst delaying close friends; fan-out work lost in a crash.

What it costs

A log and workers to run; lag becomes an SLI.

Theory · 5 of 6 · the pick, and what it gives up

E, after B, with the line measured

What the pick buys

A feed open that costs one memory read and a small merge. Writes bounded per post. Deletes that are one write. A view that can be rebuilt.

What it gives up

Strict freshness: seconds of eventual consistency. Simplicity: two paths, a tier per author, and a number to defend.

When is B enough? Until the merge on a cache miss breaks the 200 ms budget at peak. Measure it, then move.

V2 · the fan-out worker

def on_post_created(evt):
    if evt.tier == "pull":                 # celebrity: one write
        celeb_recent.lpush(evt.author, evt.post_id, trim=100)
    else:
        for page in graph.followers(evt.author, page_size=1000):
            active = [f for f in page if last_seen(f) > now() - DAYS_30]
            if evt.tier == "mid":

            timelines.add_many(active, evt.post_id, trim=500)

def get_feed(viewer, cursor, limit=50):
    ids = timelines.get(viewer)                           # ~500 IDs, ~5 ms
    for author in graph.followed_celebrities(viewer):     # a handful
        ids += celeb_recent.get(author)
    blocked = blocks.get(viewer)
    cand = dedupe(i for i in ids if i.author not in blocked)
    cand = top_by_time(cand, 200)
    scores = ranking.score(viewer, cand, deadline_ms=80) or fallback(cand)
    page = after(cursor, sorted(cand, key=scores.get, reverse=True), scores)[:limit]
    posts = [p for p in post_cache.multi_get(page) if visible(p, viewer)]
    return posts, make_cursor(page[-1], scores)

Challenge 1 of 8 · the celebrity post

What does one post cost?

Naive answer“The fan-out workers push it to every follower. We add more workers.”
BreakThe fan-out fleet and the log partition: one author's post blocks everyone else's.
Challenge 1 of 8 · the trace

Fifteen minutes for one post

20:00:00@star (110 M followers) posts
20:00:00110 M inserts at the fleet's 120,000 a second  =  about 15 minutes
20:00:30@ana posts to her 300 friends; her event waits behind @star's
20:14:10@ana's close friends see her post 14 minutes late
20:31:00@star posts again: the next quarter of an hour is gone
Label the numbersIllustrative. Even at ten times the fleet capacity, one post costs ninety seconds of all of it.
Challenge 1 of 8 · the fix

Don't fan out celebrities: pull them at read time

MechanismThe event carries the author's tier. Above the threshold the worker writes one entry to the author's recent list. Readers merge the few celebrities they follow.
PropertyL1 for everyone else: the log is no longer held by one author. L2 for readers: a few extra cache reads, budgeted.
Challenge 1 of 8 · sequence

One write on publish, a small merge on read

Challenge 1 of 8 · why it works

The cost moved to where it is small

Push the celebrityPull the celebrity
Per post110 M inserts, ~15 min of the fleet1 write
Per opennothing extra1 cache read per followed celebrity, ~0.5 ms
Who paysevery other author, in delaythe reader, inside the budget
WhyPull costs a small read multiplied by opens; push costs a write multiplied by followers. For the tail, the read is far cheaper. The hot lists are replicated to many cache nodes.
Interviewer · the second-level push
“New Year's Eve: ten times the normal posting rate, all ordinary accounts, for ten minutes.”
Pause and answer before you continue
Challenge 1 of 8 · the second fix, and the honest limit

Priority lanes, and a pull that covers lag

Priority lanes

Fan out to close-tie followers first (an interaction score), the rest after. Close friends keep their seconds.

Spread the burst

The log is keyed by author, so a burst of many authors spreads across partitions and workers.

Pull covers lag

Lag is a metric per lane. While the general lane is behind, the read merge can pull recent posts of a reader's top ties.

Honest limit: for ten minutes, the “a minute generally” target may slip. Close ties keep their seconds.
If asked, I'd say
“One celebrity post would be a hundred million writes, minutes of fan-out that block everyone else. So above a follower threshold I don't fan out at all: the post goes into a small recent-posts list for that author, and each reader merges the few celebrities they follow at read time. That costs a few milliseconds per open, which I've budgeted.”

Challenge 2 of 8 · the second fix, and the honest limit

Treat the tail of readers like the tail of authors

Precompute their merge

For readers above ~50 pulled authors, a background job merges their celebrity lists every few minutes into their timeline. Their open is one read again.

Cap the live merge

At read time, merge only the top 50 celebrities by interaction. The rest arrive with the next background merge.

Honest limit: for those readers, celebrity posts may be a few minutes late. Say it.
If asked, I'd say
“I set the line from both sides: one post's fan-out must finish inside the freshness target, and the average reader should pull only a handful of authors. That puts it around ten thousand followers, as a config value I'd tune with data. Crossing it needs no backfill because the read merge covers recent posts, and the rare reader who follows hundreds of celebrities gets their merge precomputed.”

Challenge 4 of 8 · the fix

A set keyed by post ID, and a cursor

def make_cursor(last_id, scores):
    return encode({"s": scores[last_id], "p": last_id})

def after(cursor, ranked, scores):
    if cursor is None:
        return ranked
    c = decode(cursor)
    # strictly after the last item shown, whatever arrived above it
    return [i for i in ranked if (scores[i], i) < (c["s"], c["p"])]
PropertyA re-delivered insert into a set keyed by post ID changes nothing: duplicates are impossible by data structure. Gaps are bounded by fan-out lag, which is measured.

What costs points

Design

Picking push or pull and defending it. Bodies in timelines. No number on the threshold. Strong consistency for a feed.

Correctness

Chasing deletes into copies. An offset for pagination. “Exactly once” fan-out.

Operations

Ranking with no deadline. No plan for a lost shard. Watching uptime, not freshness.

Focus

Going deep on the ranking model. Never asking about the follower distribution.
