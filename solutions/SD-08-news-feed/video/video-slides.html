Clarifying question 1 of 8
How many users, and how many open the app?
1 billion users, 500 million daily active.
Clarifying question 2 of 8
Read to write?
Feed reads are about 100 times post writes.
Clarifying question 3 of 8
How many accounts does a user follow, and how is it distributed?
Average about 200. On the other side: some accounts have over 100 million followers.
Clarifying question 4 of 8
Latency, and ranked or chronological?
p99 under 200 ms for the first page. Ranked; a scoring service exists, treat it as a black box.
Clarifying question 5 of 8
How fresh?
Seconds for close follows; a minute is tolerable generally.
Clarifying question 6 of 8
How deep?
The first 50 posts on open, paginated after.
Clarifying question 7 of 8
Content?
Text and images. Video is out of scope.
Clarifying question 8 of 8
Which is worse: seeing a post twice, or missing one?
Missing a close friend's post is worse. A duplicate on screen is a bug.

Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is
A materialised view per reader: a bounded, ranked list of recent post IDs, part precomputed, part fetched, filtered and hydrated within the budget.
Theory · 1 of 6 · what it is not

Four things it is confused with

Not the source of truth

Posts are. The view may be wrong for seconds, and can be thrown away and rebuilt.

Not a chat inbox

An inbox may never lose a message. A feed may leave out an old post, and that is acceptable.

Not search

It answers one query shape: recent, followed, ranked. Another shape needs another path.

Not the ranking model

Ranking is a stage with a budget, owned elsewhere.

Theory · 2 of 6 · the two properties

State safety and liveness separately

Safety · must never happen

S1 A viewer sees content they must not see (deleted, from a blocked account, outside its audience) for longer than 5 s.
S2 A post is lost: the source of truth is durable even if every view is lost.

Liveness · must eventually happen

L1 A post reaches its active followers' feeds within 5 s, p95.
L2 Every feed open answers within 200 ms, degraded if it must.
L3 A lost or skipped timeline is rebuilt on its next open.

Theory · 3 of 6 · the central tension

Write amplification against read cost

Theory · 4 of 6 · mechanism 1 of 7

Fan-out on write into bounded ID lists

Property it protects

L1 and L2: fresh, and one read per open.

How it works

On each post, insert (post ID, author, time) into each active follower's sorted list, trimmed to 500. Keyed by post ID, so a repeat is a no-op.

Failure it rules out

A feed open that touches 200 authors; duplicate entries when an event is delivered twice.

What it costs

120,000 inserts a second, a memory tier, and amplification on big authors.

PostgreSQL by post ID; the author index in Cassandra

How it is builtEach shard is a PostgreSQL primary with a synchronous standby in another zone; the post row and an outbox row go in one transaction. The author index is a Cassandra table keyed by author, newest first.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
PostgreSQL 16, sharded by post ID; Cassandra for the author indexWritten once, read by ID, never lost: one transaction for post plus outbox. The author index is a newest-first range per author, which Cassandra does natively.
One PostgreSQL primaryFine at MVP scale. At 600 posts a second plus the reads of 90,000 feed opens, it is one failure domain and one vacuum problem.
DynamoDB for everythingGood at both access paths, but the post-and-outbox transaction becomes a transactional write across items and tables, and we lose the plain SQL for moderation queries.
Cassandra for posts tooLeaderless writes suit the index. For the post of record, no multi-row transaction with the outbox means the dual-write bug returns.

OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not

OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
Redis Cluster, a sorted set per viewer, partitioned by viewerA feed open is one range read. A sorted set gives idempotent inserts, ordering by time and cheap trimming, and losing one is a latency problem, not data loss.
A relational table per viewer60,000 inserts a second into an indexed table is write amplification we do not need for something we can rebuild.
A Cassandra row per viewerDurable, but every feed open becomes a disk read. Fine as a cold tier, wrong for the hot path.
MemcachedNo sorted structure: every insert becomes a read-modify-write of a whole list, which races under concurrent fan-out.

How it is builtThe service reads the timeline, reads the celebrity lists of the authors the viewer follows, merges, filters blocked and deleted posts, asks ranking, and hydrates fifty posts. Each downstream call gets what is left of a 200 millisecond budget. If ranking misses its deadline the page is served in time order.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
Go services on Kubernetes, gRPC inside, a deadline propagatedThe feed open has a 200 millisecond p99 across five dependencies. A deadline passed down every call and a fallback at each hop is how it stays inside the budget.

Technology · graph and ranking

Two services we call and do not own, behind a contract

How it is builtThe graph answers followers for fan-out and followees for reads, with blocks. We cache its answers briefly and treat a block as a correctness input, checked on read. Ranking receives about 200 candidates and returns scores inside 80 milliseconds. Each has a circuit breaker and a defined fallback: cached followees for the graph, time order for ranking.
OptionWhy it is chosen, or why not
gRPC contracts with a deadline, a cache and a fallback eachBoth are another team's system on another schedule. A contract and a fallback keep their bad day from becoming ours.
Rebuilding the graph inside the feedA second copy of the follow data that would drift from the first. Own the contract, not the data.
No fallback for rankingThe feed's availability would be the ranker's availability.
