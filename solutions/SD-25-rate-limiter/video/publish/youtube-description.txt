A rate limiter sounds like a counter and an if statement. At three million requests a second over five hundred servers in three regions, with one millisecond to decide, the counter has to live somewhere, and wherever it lives it sits on every request. A Staff-bar (L6/L7) reference solution run like a real round: the design starts as a C4 container view and evolves only because an interviewer breaks it, six times, with each mechanism and technology introduced at the moment it is needed. 66 minutes, 0 numbered slides.

The thesis: a rate limiter sits on every request, so its availability and latency matter more than its accuracy. Decide in memory from a token bucket, lease the global budget to where the traffic lands, and know exactly how wrong you can be.

IN THIS VIDEO
• Estimates from one scale line: 1,000 a minute over 500 servers is 2 per server per minute, and a round trip at p99 is over the whole 1 ms budget
• V1 → V2 → V3 → V4 on one C4 container view, with component zooms of the limiter for every fix
• Six challenges: the fixed-window boundary burst, taking the round trip off the hot path, the quota service going down, one quota across three regions, a 10× burst every hour, and a customer limited while under its limit
• Mechanisms where they are needed: the token bucket in one atomic script, leases sized by demand, static stability to a bounded floor, regional budgets, staged policy versions, a false-limit SLI
