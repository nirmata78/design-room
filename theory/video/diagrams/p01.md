# Part 1 · diagram specifications

Five figures. Write them in `theory/video/diagrams_p01.py`, one function each, output to
`theory/video/svg/<name>.svg`. General rules for every figure in this course are in
`diagrams/README.md`. Charts here are plain SVG strings built in Python (no plotting library).

## p1-load-curve
- **Kind:** line chart, canvas 1600 × 760.
- **Axes:** x = hour of day, ticks at 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24. y = requests a second, ticks at 0,
  1,200, 3,600, 12,000 (use a broken axis or a compressed top band so that 12,000 fits; label the break).
- **Curve (blue, 5 px):** smooth line through (0 h, 600) (4 h, 350) (8 h, 700) (12 h, 1,050)
  (16 h, 1,150) (18 h, 1,300) (19 h, 2,400) (21 h, 3,600) (23 h, 1,200) (24 h, 600). Above 1,200
  from about 17 h to 23 h; the curve's own mean is about 1,150. (Author fix, 2026-11-13: the first
  points stayed above the average for about 13 hours.)
- **Dashed grey horizontal line** at 1,200, labelled "average · 1,200 /s".
- **Dotted red horizontal line** at 3,600, labelled "daily peak · 3× average".
- **Spike (red, 5 px):** a narrow triangle at 14 h rising to 12,000 and back within about 20 minutes
  of x, labelled "burst · 10× for minutes".
- **Shaded band (light red)** under the curve wherever it is above the 1,200 line, with the note
  "over the average for about six hours a day".

## p1-latency-ladder
- **Kind:** horizontal bar chart on a log scale, canvas 1600 × 760.
- **x axis (log):** decade ticks labelled 100 ns, 1 µs, 10 µs, 100 µs, 1 ms, 10 ms, 100 ms.
- **Six bars, top to bottom, each ending at its value, value printed at the bar end:**
  1. Memory access — 100 ns
  2. Read from a flash drive — 100 µs
  3. Round trip in one datacentre — 0.5 ms
  4. Seek on a spinning disk — 10 ms
  5. Round trip between regions, one continent — 30 ms
  6. Round trip across an ocean — 100–150 ms (bar to 120 ms, label "100–150 ms")
- Bars 1–3 grey-blue, bar 4 amber, bars 5–6 red.
- **Note box, bottom right:** "one ocean crossing = 240 datacentre round trips".

## p1-series-parallel
- **Kind:** two block diagrams side by side with a vertical divider, canvas 1600 × 700. Use `c4svg`
  boxes or plain SVG rectangles.
- **Left half, title "In series: multiply availabilities":** `Request` → box `A 99.9%` → box
  `B 99.9%` → box `C 99.9%` → `Response`, left to right. Under it, large text
  "0.999 × 0.999 × 0.999 = 99.7%" and smaller "26 hours down a year".
- **Right half, title "In parallel: multiply unavailabilities":** `Request` → a fork to two stacked
  boxes `Replica 1 · 99%` and `Replica 2 · 99%` → join → `Response`. Under it, large text
  "1 − (0.01 × 0.01) = 99.99%" and smaller "53 minutes down a year · if they fail independently".
- The words "if they fail independently" in red.

## p1-utilisation-curve
- **Kind:** line chart, canvas 1600 × 760.
- **Axes:** x = utilisation, ticks at 0, 50%, 70%, 80%, 90%, 95%, 100%. y = "time in system, as a
  multiple of service time", ticks at 1, 2, 5, 10, 20.
- **Curve (blue, 5 px):** y = 1 ÷ (1 − x), drawn from x = 0 to x = 0.96.
- **Four marked points with labels:** (50%, 2×) (80%, 5×) (90%, 10×) (95%, 20×). Dot plus label.
- **Green shaded vertical band** from 50% to 70%, labelled "size for this at peak".
- **Red shaded vertical band** from 90% to 100%, labelled "no room for a surprise".

## p1-fanout
- **Kind:** tree, canvas 1600 × 720.
- **Top:** box `Client`, arrow down to box `Root server`.
- **Middle:** from `Root server`, fan of arrows down to a row of leaf boxes. Draw 12 small leaf
  boxes with an ellipsis in the middle and the label "100 leaves, asked in parallel" above the row.
  Eleven leaves green and labelled "10 ms" (label only a few), one leaf red and labelled "1 s".
- **Right side note box:** three lines — "each leaf: slow 1 time in 100" · "all fast: 0.99¹⁰⁰ = 37%" ·
  "at least one slow: **63%**" (last line bold red).
- **Bottom caption:** "The root waits for the slowest leaf."
